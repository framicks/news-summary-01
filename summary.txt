World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026: Russia and Ukraine agreed their largest prisoner swap after two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi, numbers due Thursday; no ceasefire or deal (Reuters). World leaders opened the COP31 summit in Antalya, where 102 heads of state and government are due to speak; the US stayed away for a second year (Reuters). A tanker caught fire after hitting a naval mine in the Strait of Hormuz, Iran's army said, as the UK maritime agency reported another incident; Brent crude settled at $92.76 (Reuters). Indonesia's Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka tendered his resignation to President Prabowo, his aide told Reuters, after a court annulled his school certificate (Reuters). The US House voted 211-204 to send impeachment articles against Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively ending the effort in this Congress (NPR). US prosecutors charged three Iranian-backed men over nearly 20 alleged plots, including at RAF Fairford; Treasury Secretary Bessent moved to a military base after threats (USA Today). British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer near Gloucester on suspicion of spying for Russia; he served with NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps and has not been charged (AP). Democrat Scott Colom won Mississippi's Senate race, the AP called on Wednesday, becoming the state's first Black senator since 1881 and first Democrat in the Senate since 1982 (AP). Saudi air defences intercepted Houthi missiles and drones aimed at Riyadh and Mecca (Reuters), and Israeli forces stepped up strikes across southern Lebanon (QNA). The WHO said the Ebola outbreak in DR Congo had reached almost 11,000 cases and more than 5,300 deaths across eight provinces, with about 3,000 recovered (WHO). Some 6,300 Italian schools were evacuated after emailed bomb threats, also sent to schools in Scotland, Northern Ireland and Ireland; PM Giorgia Meloni said they posed no danger (Reuters).
